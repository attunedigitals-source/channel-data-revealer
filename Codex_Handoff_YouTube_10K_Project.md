Core lesson:
**A documentary is not a collection of facts. It is a sequence of questions, discoveries, complications, and answers.**

**Facts are ingredients. Story is how you arrange them.**

**QUESTION → INVESTIGATION → COMPLICATION → DISCOVERY → EXPLANATION → PAYOFF**

Seven-beat structure:
1. Cold Open
2. Big Question
3. Context
4. Investigation
5. Complication
6. Discovery / Explanation
7. Final Payoff

Additional principles:
- Use legitimate open loops
- Escalate
- Establish stakes
- Pair narration with visual storytelling
- Don't reveal the entire answer in the intro
- Don't manufacture mystery
- Make the payoff feel earned

First 30-second structure:
- 0–5 sec: Visual Hook
- 5–12 sec: Strange Fact/Event/Claim
- 12–20 sec: Question
- 20–30 sec: Promise

## 1. Working Title
How Did Ancient Egyptians Achieve Such Precise Stonework?

## 2. Core Question
How did ancient craftsmen shape, hollow, and joint crystalline igneous rocks (rose granite, diorite, basalt) using stone pounders, copper blades, and mineral abrasives without steel or rotary power machinery?

## 3. Why Does It Matter?
Hard igneous rocks like Aswan granite have a Mohs hardness of 6 to 7—harder than copper and bronze tools available in the Bronze Age. The original wording said this "proves" human engineering and "directly dispels" lost-technology claims.

Editorial instruction:
Avoid making the documentary an ideological argument. Let the evidence show what ancient craftsmen could accomplish.

## 4. Cold Open
Visual:
Extreme macro shot of a heavy spherical dolerite pounder striking crystalline pink granite in slow motion; fine quartz dust explodes into raking sunlight across a tightly jointed megalithic seam.

Narration:
"This stone block was shaped more than 4,500 years ago. Its surface is remarkably flat, and its seams are fitted with sub-millimeter precision."

Research flags:
- Verify exact weight distribution for Aswan dolerite mauls.
- Cite Petrie's measured survey tolerances rather than the popular "razor blade cannot enter" claim.

## 5. Big Question
Visual:
Pull back from microscopic joint to reveal colossal King's Chamber granite sarcophagus and unfinished obelisk in Aswan.

Narration:
"Yet the people who carved it possessed no iron, no hardened steel, and no diamond saws. So how did ancient Egyptian craftsmen achieve this level of precision with the tools available to them? To answer that, we have to look past the finished monuments and into the toolmarks left behind in the ancient quarries."

## 6. Context
During the Old and Middle Kingdoms (c. 2600–1800 BCE), Egyptian state projects quarried millions of tons of limestone and thousands of tons of hard igneous stone. Metallurgy was limited to arsenical copper and bronze—alloys far softer than quartz crystals in granite.

Research flag:
Verify primary archaeological/geological estimates for total monumental granite tonnage.

## 7. Investigation
Experimental archaeologists, notably Denys Stocks and Mark Lehner, are associated with experimental/archaeological work relevant to ancient stoneworking.

Three proposed/workflow areas:
1. Percussive fracturing using spherical dolerite pounders
2. Tubular and flat saw cutting using copper blades with quartz sand abrasive
3. Surface dressing using reference planes and red ochre pigment

Research flag:
Distinguish physical tool finds from modern experimental reconstructions.

## 8. Complication
"The Abrasive Paradox":
Quartz sand has Mohs hardness around 7, comparable to quartz in granite.

Questions:
- How could abrasive sand contribute to cutting hard stone?
- How were circular core drills stabilized without modern drill presses?

Research flag:
Determine what is archaeologically demonstrated versus modern experimental explanation.

## 9. Discovery / Evidence
Proposed content:
Microscopic striation analysis of Petrie's drill core No. 7 and unfinished quarry trenches may inform explanations involving abrasive grains, pressure, and material removal.

Additional hypothesis:
Guide blocks may have stabilized drill stems.

Research flags:
- Review debate over rolling vs embedded quartz-grain attrition.
- Verify whether guide blocks are archaeological artifacts or experimental hypotheses.

## 10. Explanation
Proposed three-part model:
1. Dolerite pounding
2. Copper blades + abrasive slurry
3. Surface calibration/finishing

Research flags:
- Verify Denys Stocks' experimental cutting rates and conditions.
- Verify whether the "three-rod triangulation" method is attested in ancient Egypt or is a later/modern concept.

What appeared to be impossible engineering is revealed as an extraordinary mastery of physics, mineral friction, and organized craft labor repeated across generations."

# 18. DAY 6 — CURRENT LESSON

Title:
**Documentary Research & Fact Verification**

Core principle:
**Interesting ≠ true. Popular ≠ verified. Plausible ≠ proven.**

Research chain:
**STORY QUESTION → CLAIM → SOURCE → EVIDENCE → VERIFICATION → CONFIDENCE LEVEL → NARRATION**

## Six evidence categories

1. Established Evidence
2. Strongly Supported Interpretation
3. Disputed Interpretation
4. Experimental Reconstruction
5. Unverified Claim
6. Claim to Exclude

Critical distinction:
**Can work experimentally ≠ definitely was done historically.**

## Evidence vs interpretation

Evidence:
What do we actually have?

Interpretation:
What do researchers think it means?

Narration:
What can we responsibly tell the audience?

## Source hierarchy

Tier 1:
- Archaeological excavation reports
- Scientific papers
- Original experimental studies
- Museum catalogues/collections
- Government/institutional archaeological documentation
- Original historical texts where appropriate

Tier 2:
- Peer-reviewed papers
- Academic books
- University publications
- Scholarly archaeological research

Tier 3:
- Major museums
- Established science/history publications
- Reputable documentary organizations
- University educational resources

Tier 4:
General internet sources; useful for leads but not automatically strong evidence.

Tier 5:
Social media/other YouTube videos; useful for discovering claims, not automatic evidence.

## Three-source rule

For important claims, aim for at least three independent sources when practical.

Three websites repeating one original claim are not three independent confirmations.

## Research behavior

Do not search for confirmation.

Bad:
"Proof that ancient Egyptians used quartz sand"

Better:
"ancient Egyptian stone cutting quartz abrasive experimental archaeology"

Bad:
"Petrie drill core proves copper saw"

Better:
"Petrie drill core 7 Egyptian stone drilling analysis"

Goal:
Find evidence, not confirmation.
